# Figure legends — CSDI Digital Twin vs TabPFN-3, prospective cohort (n = 250 ICSI/IVF cycles)

Specifications: 300 dpi PNG + vector PDF (embedded Type 42 fonts), 149–179 mm wide
(full journal column width), Okabe-Ito colour-blind-safe palette, sans-serif 7–8 pt.
Blue = CSDI Digital Twin throughout; orange = TabPFN-3 throughout.

Source data: `analysis_v2.py`; figures regenerated by `figures.py`.
Outcome zeros restored from the laboratory master table (22 cycles with 0 blastocysts,
53 with 0 good-quality blastocysts, 215 with 0 vitrified embryos). MII excluded from all
figures because the Digital Twin receives the observed MII count as an input
(`dt_mii_pred` ≡ observed in 100% of records) and therefore does not forecast it.

---

## Figure 1. Calibration of predicted embryo counts.

Observed versus predicted counts for (**A**) 2PN zygotes, (**B**) blastocysts and
(**C**) good-quality blastocysts. Cycles were grouped into six bins of equal size by
predicted value; points show bin means with standard errors of the observed mean. The
dashed grey line denotes perfect calibration. Axes are drawn to a common scale with
equal aspect so that departures from the identity line are directly readable.

At the zygote stage both models track the identity line closely. At both blastocyst
stages the CSDI curve is almost flat (slope +0.25 for blastocysts), indicating that
predictions vary over a wide range while observed counts do not — the model separates
cycles poorly and sits far above the identity line. TabPFN-3 lies closer to the identity
line but likewise shows limited slope.

---

## Figure 2. Bland–Altman agreement by stage and model.

Difference between predicted and observed counts plotted against their mean, for CSDI
(**A–C**, top row) and TabPFN-3 (**D–F**, bottom row) across 2PN zygotes, blastocysts and
good-quality blastocysts. Solid coloured line = mean bias; dotted lines = 95% limits of
agreement (bias ± 1.96 SD); dashed grey line = zero difference. Points are jittered by
±0.11 to expose overplotting of integer counts. Vertical scales are shared within each
column so the two models are directly comparable.

CSDI shows a large positive bias at both blastocyst stages (+3.23 and +2.33 embryos)
with limits of agreement excluding zero over most of the range, and a pronounced
proportional trend: the diagonal bands arise from cycles in which no blastocyst was
obtained, where the difference equals the prediction itself. TabPFN-3 is close to
unbiased for blastocysts (+0.11) and modestly negative for good-quality blastocysts
(−0.59). At the zygote stage both models are near-unbiased, but TabPFN-3 has markedly
wider limits of agreement (−6.4 to +6.0 versus −3.6 to +4.3).

## Figure 3. Predictive-uncertainty calibration.

(**A**) Empirical coverage of nominal prediction intervals with 95% Clopper–Pearson
confidence intervals. Orange = TabPFN-3 P10–P90 (nominal 80%, orange reference line);
blue = CSDI P2.5–P97.5 (nominal 95%, blue reference line). CSDI intervals are provided
only for blastocyst stages. (**B**) Pinball (quantile) loss of the TabPFN-3 predictive
distribution at each quantile level, by stage.

TabPFN-3 intervals are well calibrated at the zygote stage (78.4%, 95% CI 72.8–83.3,
against a nominal 80%) and for vitrified embryos (78.4%), and conservative for
blastocysts (88.8%). CSDI 95% intervals cover only 60.8% (95% CI 54.4–66.9) of observed
blastocyst counts — a shortfall of 34 percentage points — indicating substantial
overconfidence.

---

## Figure 4. Point-forecast error before and after linear recalibration.

Mean absolute error for each model in raw form (pale bars) and after leave-one-out
linear recalibration of predictions onto observed counts (saturated bars). Horizontal
black lines mark the error of a trivial constant forecast equal to the cohort median.

Recalibration removes 79% of the CSDI blastocyst error (3.40 → 0.73 embryos) and closes
almost the entire gap to TabPFN-3 (0.71), demonstrating that the apparent failure of
CSDI at the blastocyst stage is a scale-calibration artefact of a model tuned to a
population with a threefold higher blastulation rate (predicted 0.73 versus observed
0.21), not an absence of biological signal. Critically, at both blastocyst stages
neither model — recalibrated or not — outperforms the constant baseline, whereas at the
zygote stage both models improve substantially on it (CSDI 1.44 versus 3.21).
