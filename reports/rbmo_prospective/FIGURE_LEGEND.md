---

Difference between the Digital Twin and TabPFN predicted probability of clinical pregnancy,
plotted against their mean, shown separately for each centre (**A–D**) because pregnancy
rates range from 26% to 70% between centres. Solid line = mean bias with 95% CI (dark band);
dotted lines = 95% limits of agreement (bias ± 1.96 SD) with 95% CIs (grey bands; Bland &
Altman 1999); dash-dot line = regression of the difference on the mean; dashed grey = zero.

Mean bias was small in every centre: −5.7 pp (95% CI −6.8 to −4.6) at Centre A, +0.8 pp
(−0.3 to +2.0) at Centre B, −2.8 pp (−4.6 to −1.1) at Centre C and +1.1 pp (−2.6 to +4.7)
in the fourth centre. The limits of agreement were wide, spanning 36–48 pp: −28.5 to +17.1
(A), −23.1 to +24.7 (B), −24.6 to +18.9 (C) and −17.5 to +19.6 (fourth centre, n = 28, with
wide CIs). The two models therefore agree on average but can differ by 20–28 pp for an
individual patient. In Centre B the disagreement depended strongly on the predicted level
(slope −0.35, 95% CI −0.46 to −0.23; r = 0.44 between models): in cycles with a low mean
estimate, the Digital Twin exceeded TabPFN by up to 60 pp. In the other centres the slope was
small and positive (A +0.13, 95% CI +0.06 to +0.20). Within centres, disagreement did not
depend on the observed outcome (pregnant minus non-pregnant +0.2 pp, p = 0.79).

The pooled bias (−2.4 pp, 95% CI −3.2 to −1.7; sensitivity analysis only) averages over
centre-specific biases of opposite sign and should not be reported as the agreement estimate.
Agreement between the models says nothing about accuracy: in Centre C both predicted about
47–50% against an observed rate of 26%.
