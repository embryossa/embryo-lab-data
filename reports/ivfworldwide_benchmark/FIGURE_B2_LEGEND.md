# Figure B2. Bland–Altman agreement of predicted clinical-pregnancy probability: Digital Twin vs TabPFN.

Public benchmark cohort, n = 489 cycles (FRESH 299, FET_FC 190; 142 pregnancies, 29.0%).
Source data: `data/processed/benchmark/dt_predictions.csv` (`DIGITAL TWIN`, `PRAI`, `Preg`);
figure and statistics regenerated by `benchmark_bland_altman.py` → `bench_B4_bland_altman.csv`.
Specifications: 300 dpi PNG + vector PDF (Type 42 fonts), 180 mm wide. Point colour and marker
shape both encode the observed outcome (colour-blind-validated pair).

---

Difference between the Digital Twin ensemble and TabPFN predicted probabilities plotted against
their mean, (**A**) on the probability scale (percentage points) and (**B**) on the logit scale,
where the difference equals the log odds ratio between the two models. Solid line = mean bias with
its 95% CI (dark band); dotted lines = 95% limits of agreement (bias ± 1.96 SD) with their 95% CIs
(grey bands; Bland & Altman 1999 approximation); dash-dot line = least-squares regression of the
difference on the mean (proportional bias); dashed grey line = zero difference.

On the probability scale the Digital Twin predicts systematically higher than TabPFN: mean bias
+10.0 pp (95% CI 8.7 to 11.2), limits of agreement −17.5 pp (−19.7 to −15.4) to +37.4 pp
(35.3 to 39.6); 94.1% of cycles fall within the limits. There is no proportional bias on this
scale (slope −0.05, 95% CI −0.14 to +0.04; p = 0.25), but the differences are right-skewed
(Shapiro–Wilk p < 0.001), with a tail of cycles in the 30–50% range where the Digital Twin
exceeds TabPFN by 40–64 pp. On the logit scale the bias corresponds to an odds ratio of 1.58
(95% LoA 0.40 to 6.22), and a significant negative slope (−0.20, 95% CI −0.30 to −0.10;
p < 0.001) shows that relative disagreement is greatest where both models predict low
probabilities.

The disagreement is not related to the observed outcome (bias +10.3 pp in pregnant vs +9.8 pp
in non-pregnant cycles; Welch p = 0.75), but it differs between cohorts: +13.2 pp
(11.6 to 14.9) in FRESH versus +4.8 pp (3.2 to 6.5) in FET_FC cycles.

Bland–Altman analysis quantifies agreement between the two models, not accuracy: both models'
mean predictions (Digital Twin 49.7%, TabPFN 39.7%) exceed the observed pregnancy rate (29.0%);
calibration against the outcome is reported separately (Figure B, Table B1).
